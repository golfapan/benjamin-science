Use the two-hour sitting flexibly

Reserve about ten minutes for short breaks, transitions and records. Use the remaining time for the next reading/question block, due review and correction. Full written working for selected problems belongs in the first-attempt time; teacher discussion and repair use the correction allowance. Stop at two hours and resume unfinished practice next time. There is no extra homework allowance hidden in this plan.

When work takes longer

  1. Keep the model/condition teaching and correction. Use the exact gap to choose a short repair; do not repeat a familiar direct drill more than needed.
  2. Swap a selected Stretch for the needed Foundation/Core repair; Circuits or Mechanics Stretch can replace one of the default four. Preserve the CV bridge if Gas Stretch is removed.
  3. If more time is needed, defer an extra Core/Stretch page or postpone a test until its model is ready. Check first-use knowledge before removing a page.
  4. If two consecutive weekly budgets still overrun after swaps, carry the unfinished work into another week. Keep a final delayed-review date; do not shorten an attempt or invent completion to fit the calendar.

Written method · a small amount of evidence each sitting

Choose one or two substantial problems per sitting for full written reasoning. Keep other routine working brief. The chapter’s five or six scored questions remain unchanged. Oral explanations are separate, unscored discussion.

  1. System and sketch: show the object, boundary, ray, circuit or graph that matters.
  2. Given and unknown: label quantities with units, signs and any constraints.
  3. Model and reason: name the law or evidence model, say why it applies and state the needed assumption.
  4. Working: show the non-obvious rearrangement or linked steps, then calculate with units.
  5. Check: test size, direction, units, a limiting case or an independent observation.

Keep the original first answer visible. Add corrections alongside it, dated, with the exact hint or assistance used. A final correct number alone does not establish a sound method.

Oral check · choose two prompts, about 2–3 minutes

Ask the learner to explain why the model applies and predict a changed condition. Listen before giving a cue. Record the explanation actually given; do not turn the oral check into another typed question or automatic score.

Delayed transfer · after 7–14 days

  1. Set the reference date: use the most recent targeted teaching, solution review or correction on this idea. Write a planned review date 7–14 days later. Record any intervening rehearsal.
  2. Select fresh work: choose one or two problems, about 10–15 minutes in total. Use an unused course task or a teacher-written variant with a changed unknown, boundary, observation or representation. Check it uses already-taught principles. A number-only substitution is recall practice, not sufficient transfer evidence.
  3. Protect reserved papers: do not take individual questions from a full paper reserved for an unseen mock. A task is fresh only if the learner has not already seen its question or solution.
  4. Freeze the first attempt: no notes, hints or solutions before that attempt. Record actual date, elapsed time and breaks, the first answer and the written model. If stuck, save that work before helping.
  5. Correct and decide: log the precise cue and the corrected work separately. If the model was selected independently, check the method and explanation. If a model cue was needed, repair that link and plan a different variant after another delay.

The 7–14-day window is this course’s review routine, not a validated mastery threshold. Record the actual interval if different. Use a later fresh problem after correction; an immediate repeat is not a delayed check. Use the due-review allowance for earlier ideas; the final 30–60-minute follow-up is outside Route A's 40-hour base.

Teacher interpretation · use observed evidence
  • Independent method shown: the observer confirms the attempt conditions; the learner selects and explains the model, carries out coherent working and checks it. Record any arithmetic error separately; “method shown” need not mean the numerical answer was correct.
  • With a cue: the learner can complete or explain the work after a recorded model/condition hint. Keep this separate from independent evidence.
  • Repair needed: the central model or condition remains unclear. Choose that specific concept for repair.
  • Evidence incomplete: only a number is available, the question was already seen, or the help conditions are unknown. Do not fill in a mastery judgment.

Classify the gap as knowledge, model/assumption, graph/data, algebra, units/signs or time. Keep numerical correctness and independence as separate observations. There is no automatic promotion rule or added course mark here.

Simple investigations with ordinary equipment

Choose a safe low-risk investigation: measure cooling water with an appropriate thermometer; compare pendulum periods at small amplitude; use a school microscope image and calibration; or classify leaves using an observed-trait key. State a question, control variables, repeat readings, tabulate measurements, plot a graph and discuss limitations. Use adult supervision for warm water or electrical equipment. Do not culture unknown microbes or handle radioactive materials.

These are practice activities. IJSO experimental preparation needs supervised equipment use and verified practical performance.
